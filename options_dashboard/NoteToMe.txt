Regime — POSITIVE γ in green or NEGATIVE γ in red, with FADE | HIGH or TREND | LOW below

How to read it for trading:

POSITIVE γ + FADE + HIGH → strong mean-reversion environment. Sell premium, fade moves toward call wall/put wall, expect price to pin near max gamma.
NEGATIVE γ + TREND + HIGH → strong trending environment. Buy breakouts, follow momentum, expect expansion away from put wall.
Spot crossing the GEX flip line = regime change signal. Watch for it closely.

Gamma sign (POSITIVE vs NEGATIVE):
Simply the sum of all net GEX across every strike. If the total is positive, dealers are net long gamma — they have to sell when price rises and buy when price falls, which stabilizes price. If negative, dealers amplify moves.
Bias (FADE vs TREND):
Directly follows the gamma sign. Positive gamma = mean-reverting market = fade moves. Negative gamma = self-reinforcing market = follow the trend.

Regime vs Vanna/VIX — which to trust:
They measure fundamentally different things and operate on different timescales:
Regime tells you the structural environment — is the market set up for mean reversion or trending? It's based on the total accumulated positioning (OI across all strikes). It changes slowly — maybe once or twice a week. Think of it as the weather forecast.
Vanna/VIX tells you the current flow pressure — right now, are dealer hedging flows pushing price up or down? It changes with every VIX tick. Think of it as looking out the window.
When they agree, high conviction: POSITIVE γ + FADE + BULLISH vanna/VIX = very strong mean-reversion setup, fade dips aggressively.
When they disagree, it depends on your timeframe:

Intraday scalping → follow Vanna/VIX. If VIX is spiking and vanna says BEARISH, that flow pressure is happening right now regardless of the structural regime. Get out of the way short-term.
Swing / multi-day → follow Regime. A POSITIVE γ environment will reassert itself once the VIX spike settles. The Vanna/VIX disagreement is often temporary — a brief volatility shock within a stable gamma structure.
The dangerous case → NEGATIVE γ + BEARISH vanna/VIX. Both say the same thing: dealers are amplifying downside moves AND current flows are pushing lower. This is when crashes accelerate. Respect this signal heavily.
The tricky case → POSITIVE γ + BEARISH vanna/VIX. Structural support exists but current flows are pushing against it. Usually the positive gamma wins and price snaps back — but if VIX keeps rising and the signal persists for multiple hours, it can mean the regime is about to flip. Watch whether price approaches the GEX flip line — if it crosses, the regime changes and both signals will align bearish.

In practice: use Regime for your directional bias and position sizing, use Vanna/VIX for timing entries and exits within that bias.
